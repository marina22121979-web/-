# Story-First Video Editing

AI can already make technically clean edits. What it usually lacks is **taste** — the judgment calls that separate an edit that works from an edit that moves people. This skill encodes a professional editing framework so every edit follows story logic, disciplined pacing, and intentional style instead of default assembly.

---

## Prime Directive: Story First

Every project tells a story. Nothing is cut randomly.

- Every edit — even a 30-second clip — has an arc: **hook/intro → build → climax → closing**
- If a shot doesn't serve the story, it doesn't make the cut
- Before cutting anything, identify the story being told. If it isn't clear from the footage or brief, ask the user what it is before proceeding.

---

## Step 0 — Classify the Project

Determine the project's intent first. This single decision drives everything else:

- **Sales / attention intent** (capture audience, drive action) → fast & rhythmic pacing
- **Story / film intent** (the piece exists to tell a story on its own terms) → breathing & patient pacing

When in doubt, ask which one the project is really for.

## Project Matrix

| Project type | Opening | Pacing | Cut length | Music | Captions | Closing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weddings / Events** | Cinematic | Breathing, patient | 2–4s | Story-matched, emotional | No | Fade, logo, or hard cut |
| **Event recaps** | Cinematic, strong first 3s | Per brief | 2–4s | Trending or story-matched | Optional | Logo or fade |
| **Music videos** | Cinematic, creative | Patient but rhythmic — track drives it | 2–4s, on beat | The track dictates everything | No | Hard cut or fade |
| **Brand films (story-driven)** | Cinematic | Breathing, patient | 2–4s | Story-matched | No | Logo or fade |
| **Brand promos (sales-driven)** | Hook in first 3s | Fast & rhythmic | 2–3s | Trending (reach) | Yes | Call to action |
| **Social content** | Hook in first 3s | Fast & rhythmic | 2–3s max | Trending (reach) | Yes | Call to action |
| **Interviews** | Hook in first 3s (strong quote) | Moderate | Max 5–6s per frame | Subtle, story-matched | Yes | CTA or last line of dialogue |

## Openings

**Cinematic open** — weddings, events, music videos, story-driven brand films, recaps
- Slow establishing shot, audio before picture, or a striking visual hook
- The first seconds must signal *"a story is about to be told"*
- Never open story content like an advertisement

**Fast hook** — social content, sales-driven promos, interviews
- Grab the viewer in 3 seconds or less: a text hook, a bold statement, or the single most striking moment
- Roll straight from the hook into pace — no slow warmup after the grab

---

## Pacing & Cut Discipline

- **Cut lengths (the amateur test):**
  - Social content: cut every 2–3 seconds, maximum
  - Music videos, weddings, promos, brand work: 2–4 seconds
  - Interviews: may linger, but never past 5–6 seconds unless it's a formal sit-down
- **Beat cutting:** when cutting to music, land cuts exactly on the beat — especially beat drops. An off-beat cut is the #1 marker of immature editing. Map the track's beats before fine-cutting.
- **Don't over-cut:** cutting too fast reads as beginner work and distracts from the story.

---

## Transitions

**Default:** hard cuts, timed to the beat. Also acceptable: immersive/seamless transitions, whip cuts, light flares, light leaks — when they serve the story.

**Avoid:** slides, swipes, and gimmicky preset transitions. If it ships in a "500 transitions pack," it probably cheapens the edit.

---

## Music

- **Attention-driven content** (social, sales promos): favor trending audio — reach matters
- **Story-driven content:** music matches the story's emotion — dramatic story, dramatic track; mellow story, vibey track
- The music and the story must never fight each other
- On music videos, the track dictates the entire edit rhythm

---

## Color Direction

- Match the grade to the project's emotion and genre; keep it consistent across every cut
- Aim for a cinematic finish: intentional contrast, controlled saturation — never flat, never oversaturated consumer-camera look
- Celebratory content (weddings, events): skin tones stay warm, flattering, and alive
- If the user has a signature look defined (see **Make It Yours**), apply it and present for approval before finalizing

---

## Text, Graphics & Captions

- Minimal-text philosophy: graphics earn their place only by serving the story
- Prefer creative slide-in text and purposeful inserts over decorative clutter
- Plain lower thirds are boring — use only as functional captions when required
- **Captions on:** interviews, social content, marketing content. **Captions off:** weddings, music videos.
- If a graphic isn't strong, cut it rather than ship something weak

---

## Closings

- **Marketing content:** end on a call to action — ideally delivered as the last line of dialogue
- **Story content:** logo, fade to black, or a clean hard cut — whatever the story has earned

---

## Never-Do List (instant rejection)

1. Cuts that miss the beat
2. Shots that overstay their welcome
3. Slide, swipe, or gimmick transitions
4. Over-cutting into distraction
5. Opening story content like a commercial
6. Boring lower thirds

---

## Workflow — Editing in an NLE (Palmier Pro, Premiere Pro, Resolve, CapCut, etc.)

Work in checkpoints, not one big reveal. If the agent is connected to **Palmier Pro** — the open-source editor that exposes a local MCP server for AI agents — execute these steps directly on the timeline (trimming, reordering, captioning, generating) while still pausing at each checkpoint for approval:

1. **Story pass** — review footage, define the arc (hook → build → climax → close). Confirm the story with the user before cutting.
2. **Structure rough cut** — assemble on story beats only. **Checkpoint: show the user.**
3. **Rhythm pass** — lay the track, map beats, enforce cut-length discipline, land cuts on beats.
4. **Polish pass** — transitions, graphics, captions per the rules above.
5. **Grade** — apply color direction. **Checkpoint: user approval.**
6. **Audio pass** — balance dialogue and music.
7. **Export & final review.**

If footage, brief, or music is missing at any step, ask rather than assume.
